Latch

Turn the dates buried in your messages into calendar entries and to-dos — read on your phone or PC, never on a server.

A school circular says the parent–teacher meeting is on Friday. A bill says payment is due on the 20th. You read it, you mean to note it down, and you do not.

Select the text and choose Latch, or share a message, a screenshot or a PDF. Latch finds the dates, shows you what it found, and — when you say so — saves them to your own Google Calendar or Google Tasks. Photograph a business card and it becomes a contact in your own Google Contacts, after you have checked every field.

What it does

  • Reads dates in ordinary writing — written and numeric dates, ranges, times, and Hinglish such as kal, parso and 4 baje.
  • Never guesses. No date found means an undated to-do, never “today”.
  • Shows you everything before saving. Every date is a row you can untick, flip between event and to-do, or retitle. Undo is offered for ten seconds after a save.
  • Notices what you already saved, and offers to move an entry rather than make a second one when a date changes.
  • Recipes turn one date into a plan — prep three working days ahead, a follow-up the day after — skipping weekends and holidays.

Your data

Latch has no server and no account to create. Nothing you capture leaves your device except to your own Google account, and it uses your Google account only for the features above. The privacy policy says exactly what is read, what is stored, and what — if anything — goes elsewhere.

Availability

Latch is in pre-release. It is not yet on Google Play, and signing in with Google currently works only for accounts on its test list. It runs on Android 8.0 and later, and on Windows.

Latch is open source: github.com/KSYADAV1978/latch-android. It has no advertising, no in-app purchase and no paid tier.
